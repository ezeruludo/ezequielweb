My own

Doctoral thesis Masculinities, bodies and sexual orientations of men who take part in the self-managed digital sex market in Argentina. Supervised by Dr. Santiago Morcillo and Dr. Natalia Bermúdez. UNC / IDACOR · CONICET fellowship 2022–2027

This thesis is an ethnography of men who create adult content in contemporary Argentina, carried out between 2021 and 2026. It approaches pornography as a social practice and analyses how professional middle-class men become adult content creators.

Through this object, the research revisits classic anthropological problems concerning the making of the person, bodies, intimacy, sexual orientations and the very definition of the pornographic. Its central argument is that the promise of a “democratisation of porn”, characteristic of platform capitalism, widens access to the production and circulation of pornography while reorganising the hierarchies of the sex market — of gender, class, race, age and bodily ability — under the language of entrepreneurship, community and authenticity.

From this perspective, the thesis shows that professionalisation transforms the way creators perceive and inhabit their own bodies; that the public identities they produce for the market gain a relative autonomy from those who embody them; and that the promise of transgressing limits is one of the main drivers of value in a market that generates income without offering stable career paths.

Brazil Masculinities, bodies and sexualities of men who take part in the self-managed online sex market in Argentina and Brazil. Move La America programme · CAPES · Doctoral research stay 2025 · Supervised by Dr. Fernando Rabossi

In 2025 I carried out a doctoral exchange in Brazil with a CAPES Move La America fellowship, supervised by Dr. Fernando Rabossi. Although creators in both countries use many of the same platforms, they navigate different social histories, moral landscapes and market conditions.

That experience consolidated my interest in ethnographic comparison as a way of understanding how globally shared digital infrastructures are appropriated through situated forms of gender, class, age, race, sexuality and bodily ability.

Undergraduate thesis “Somos técnica, entrenamiento y salud” [“We are technique, training and health”]. An ethnography of the poetics and politics of bodies in the world of CrossFit. Supervised by Fabiola Heredia, MA, and Dr. Nicolás Cabrera. Undergraduate thesis · UNC · 2022

CrossFit is a training method created by Greg Glassman that combines bodily techniques from gymnastics, Olympic weightlifting and metabolic conditioning. It became a registered trademark in 2000; by 2017 it had more than fifteen thousand affiliated gyms, seven of them in the city of Córdoba.

Fieldwork took place in one of those gyms, where I identified three ways of identifying with the practice. Crossfitters had built markers of commitment to training into their lives: eating habits, knowledge of a wide repertoire of exercises, training styles and clothing. Coaches found a livelihood in the practice and took training courses to teach it, in some cases going on to own their own gym. Athletes professionalised their training and competed, so that much of their life came to revolve around sporting performance.

The question that organised the research was why CrossFit became a central dimension in the lives of those who identified in these three ways, and how the practice gave meaning to particular contemporary aesthetics, sensibilities and subjectivities.

Team projects

Platformisation Platformised discourses on the public-common: an interdisciplinary characterisation of the enunciative strategies used on contemporary media platforms and their flows of meaning. PID 2023–2026 · Directed by Dr. Natalia Raimondo Anselmino

The project studies what happens to discourses about public matters when they are published on platforms: whom they address, what addressee they construct and where meaning circulates. Its approach is sociosemiotic, attentive to the conditions of production and recognition rather than to the content of the message.

Within the team I follow the case of discourse on “narcoterrorism” in Rosario.

Pilot study Living conditions of people who offer sexual services in Argentina. IDB · AR-T1189 · 2021 · Interviews and qualitative analysis

I took part in piloting the instruments for a situational assessment of adults who offer sexual services in exchange for money or equivalents in Argentina, directed by Dr. Gabriela Irrazábal (CEIL-CONICET), Dr. Natalia Bermúdez (UNC-CONICET) and Dr. Inés Pérez (UNMdP-CONICET). I conducted some of the interviews and their qualitative analysis.

UXR User experience research on volunteers in Fundación TECHO’s international projects. Fundación TECHO · 2024 · Diploma in UX Research, Universidad de Belgrano

I was part of the team that researched, for Fundación TECHO, the motivations and barriers affecting volunteers’ participation and retention in its international projects. We worked with recruitment surveys, eight semi-structured in-depth interviews and affinity maps on shared boards, organising the analysis in three stages: before, during and after the trip.

The finding that shaped the report was that what attracts volunteers is not the same as what keeps them. The recommendations focused on sustained mentoring, recognition of the work done and flexibility in the length of stays.

UX research shares interviewing, observation and attention to situated use with ethnography, but it works with short deadlines and with deliverables that must be useful to someone who makes decisions. That tension, between the long time of fieldwork and the short time of the product, proved productive for thinking about platforms from inside their own logic.

En los márgenes de la “democratización del porno” de OnlyFans: visibilidades en etnografías del mercado sexual digital

[On the margins of OnlyFans’ ‘democratization of porn’: visibilities in ethnographies of the digital sex market]

Ezequiel Aguilera & Nicolás Barbeito

Revista de la Escuela de Antropología, XXXVIII

Abstract

The arrival of the platform OnlyFans transformed the landscape of the digital sex market. By providing accessible infrastructure —for those with internet access, a camera, and social network knowledge— many of its users claimed that it introduced a kind of “democratization” of pornography. However, the restrictions imposed by its terms of use draw clear boundaries around this so-called democratization, regulating which bodies, practices, and narratives are allowed to circulate.

Ezequiel Aguilera

Sudamérica: Revista de Ciencias Sociales, 22, 441–465

Abstract

The article is an exercise in reflexivity on the challenges of conducting an ethnography of male adult content creators. Within this framework, one of the strategies that enabled immersion in the field was becoming a “pornographer.” This positioning not only sought to reciprocate the time and knowledge shared by the interlocutors but also to experiment with a more horizontal anthropology, attentive to their needs and forms of collaboration.

[Becoming athletes: the sporting professionalisation of CrossFit as a bodily practice]

Ezequiel Aguilera

Etcétera. Revista del Área de Ciencias Sociales del CIFFyH, 11

Abstract

This article is the product of an ethnographic fieldwork carried out in places specializing in the bodily practice of CrossFit in the city of Córdoba (Argentina). Large international companies produced competitive events of various scales in an attempt to turn the practice of CrossFit into a sport. This made an impact in subjects who self-identified as athletes, who claimed professionalizing their sports practice by transforming various aspects of their daily lives in the search for the excellence of their performances (areté).

About

Background

I trained at the Universidad Nacional de Córdoba, where I earned my degree in Anthropology with an Honourable Mention of the University Prize. Since 2022 I have been doing my PhD in Anthropological Sciences at the same university, with a CONICET fellowship, based at the Instituto de Antropología de Córdoba (IDACOR). I am also a member of REDINSEX and of research teams based at UNC, UNR, UNSJ and UNSAM.

My interests have centred on the intersection of subjectivities, bodies and sexualities, in relation to contemporary sensibilities and large corporations in the context of globalisation. My first ethnographic work was on the CrossFit community in Córdoba, where I analysed how these bodily techniques were linked to aesthetics and sensibilities promoted by the fitness industry.

In my current research I study how men produce content in the digital sex market. Through this object, the research revisits classic anthropological problems concerning the making of the person, bodies, intimacy, sexual orientations and the very definition of the pornographic. Its central argument is that the promise of a “democratisation of porn” characteristic of platform capitalism does more than it states. Like other image technologies before it, it widens access to the production and circulation of pornography while reorganising the hierarchies of the sex market — of gender, class, race, age and bodily ability — under the language of entrepreneurship, community and authenticity.

I work mainly with ethnographic methods and interviews, as well as the analysis of visual materials, archives and digital data. In addition to my training in Anthropology, I completed a diploma in user research (UX Research), which added new tools to the way I work with digital technologies.

In 2025 I did a doctoral research stay in Brazil with a CAPES Move La America fellowship, which opened the research up to comparison. I was an honorary teaching assistant in Anthropological Theory III and a student assistant in Anthropology in Urban Contexts. My work has appeared in Antípoda, Campos, Sudamérica, RELIES, Avances and the Revista de la Escuela de Antropología, among others.
